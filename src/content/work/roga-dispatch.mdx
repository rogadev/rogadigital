## Context

In medical and assisted transport, a late ride isn't an inconvenience. It's a missed dialysis appointment, or someone stranded at a clinic door. Most small transport offices still hold the day together with a whiteboard, a spreadsheet, a group chat, and one dispatcher's memory. Then billing week comes, and someone rebuilds every trip by hand to invoice each payor.

Roga Dispatch started in 2023 as a driver app, designed and built for a medical-transport company in Nanaimo, BC: a way for its drivers to track their trips and organize their day. It has since grown into a full system for the whole office, with dispatch, billing, and driver standing built around the driver app, and a ground-up redesign is underway.

The dispatcher's screen is built to be dense and calm at the same time:

- **A live dispatch board.** Every trip, driver, and coverage gap on one screen, grouped by the hour and updated as the day moves. Problems such as an unassigned pickup or a driver running late rise to the top, so the office catches them before riders do.
- **Recurring trips that schedule themselves.** Set a standing appointment once, such as dialysis three times a week, and its trips land on the board every week. Regular riders never get re-entered by hand.
- **Zones and rate sheets.** Draw your service zones once and every trip gets an accurate fare automatically, with no per-ride math and no rate lookups.
- **Payor billing and invoicing.** Coverage and zone rates apply to every trip, then roll up into payor invoices that match what you actually drove, with your company name on every invoice.
- **Online ride requests.** A request form a transport company can embed on its own website, so new bookings arrive in the dispatcher's inbox instead of on a voicemail.

Behind the driver app sits **driver standing**: a reliability score for every driver that moves with the incidents the office logs, from an accident to a rider's compliment. When a score slips past a threshold, the system opens a disciplinary task for the office automatically, so accountability doesn't depend on anyone remembering.

## How it's built

- **Front end:** SvelteKit 2 with Svelte 5 runes, Tailwind CSS v4, and shadcn-svelte. Dispatcher and admin screens are desktop-first and information-dense; driver screens are mobile-first, with 44-pixel touch targets.
- **Data:** Neon serverless Postgres with Drizzle ORM. Every company's data is isolated by organization, and production schema changes ship only through committed migrations.
- **Auth and roles:** Better Auth with role-based access for admins, dispatchers, and drivers. One person can hold several roles.
- **Automation:** Vercel cron jobs generate recurring trips nightly and run the weekly standing assessment.
- **Hosting and monitoring:** Vercel, with Sentry for errors in both the server and the browser.
- **Quality gates:** Vitest and Playwright behind a `ready` script that runs before every change ships.
